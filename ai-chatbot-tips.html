Tips to make the most of AI chatbots

Research into how AI affects older adults is still developing, and evidence for many outcomes remains mixed or inconclusive. Even so, AI is likely to become increasingly present in everyday life. Learning to use it consciously can help us make the most of its potential benefits while limiting possible risks.

Think of AI as a tool

AI can help with learning, planning, finding information, generating ideas, and solving everyday problems. The goal does not have to be to avoid AI, but rather to use it in ways that support your own thinking, judgment, relationships, and independence.

Keep your brain involved

AI makes it easy to hand over tasks that we would otherwise have to think through ourselves. Research has linked more frequent reliance on AI with greater cognitive offloading — shifting some mental work from ourselves to a technological tool — and with lower performance on measures of critical thinking (Gerlich, 2025).

This does not mean that using AI automatically causes cognitive decline. A useful approach is to let AI support your thinking rather than replace it. Try forming your own answer first, asking the chatbot to challenge your reasoning, or comparing its answer with your own ideas.

Use AI to explore

AI can make information much easier to access. You might use it to learn about a topic you have always been curious about, understand an unfamiliar concept, compare different viewpoints, or investigate a question that would previously have required searching through many websites.

Used this way, AI can lower the effort required to begin exploring a topic and potentially encourage curiosity and continued learning.

Verify important claims

AI chatbots can produce incorrect information while sounding completely confident. These errors are often called hallucinations.

If a claim matters — particularly information about politics, health, finances, law, or other important decisions — do not rely on the chatbot's answer alone.

Follow the sources provided with the answer and check whether they actually support the claim. If no reliable source is given, verify the information independently using trustworthy sources, preferably primary, official, or academic sources where appropriate.

Ask better questions

Tell the chatbot what you are trying to accomplish and provide relevant context. You can ask it to explain something more simply, provide several possibilities, identify weaknesses in an argument, or show evidence both for and against a claim.

You can also ask: “How certain are you?”, “What evidence supports this?”, or “What are the strongest arguments against this answer?”

Use AI to support real life

AI may be most useful when what happens on the screen leads to something useful away from the screen.

For example, you could ask it to help plan an outing, prepare questions for a doctor, discover local activities, practise a difficult conversation, learn a new skill, or think of ways to connect with family, friends, and your community.

Protect your privacy

Avoid entering passwords, banking information, government identification numbers, confidential documents, highly sensitive personal information, or private information about other people without their permission.

Before sharing something personal, consider whether the chatbot really needs that information to answer your question.

Keep your own judgment

AI can provide suggestions, but it does not understand your life in the same way that you do. Treat its output as information to consider rather than an instruction that has to be followed.

For medical, legal, financial, or other high-stakes decisions, qualified professionals and reliable primary sources should remain the final authority.

Maintain healthy boundaries

Pay attention to how AI use affects your mood, habits, thinking, and relationships. Take breaks when needed and try not to let chatbot interaction replace meaningful activities or human relationships.

A chatbot can simulate conversation, but it is not a person, therapist, emergency service, or substitute for genuine human support.

✓

Key takeaway

AI does not have to do your thinking for you to be useful. The most beneficial approach may be to use it consciously, critically, and with a clear purpose — taking advantage of its ability to provide information and assistance while continuing to exercise your own judgment, curiosity, and critical thinking.

AI has potential

Successfully applying AI to improve people's lives is a real possibility across many areas. It may support access to information, learning, communication, independence, health services, and social connection.

Research still matters

At the same time, new technology should not simply be assumed to be beneficial. We need research to understand when AI helps, when it does not, who benefits, and what risks may emerge .

This is especially important for older adults, because much of the existing evidence is still limited, mixed, or inconclusive.

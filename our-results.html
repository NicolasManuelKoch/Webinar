Evidence from the study

Our results

Because only a small number of participants completed the study, these findings should be interpreted cautiously. Most outcomes did not show large or clearly distinguishable pre- to post-study differences, although several descriptive patterns are worth exploring.

Social interaction

One of the most interesting descriptive patterns appeared in participants' reports of meaningful social interactions. The control group showed a downward trend from the pre-study to the post-study survey, while the chatbot group showed an upward trend, reporting more meaningful social interactions during the 14 days preceding the post-study survey than during the comparable period before the study.

This difference is potentially interesting, but it needs to be interpreted very cautiously. The chatbot group was particularly small, meaning that chance, individual differences, or reporting biases could readily produce the observed pattern. For example, participants who knew the study was encouraging social connection may have been more likely to report social interactions after the intervention.

Loneliness and well-being

In contrast to the apparent difference in social interaction, loneliness and well-being did not appear to change substantially between the pre-study and post-study surveys in either condition.

This is noteworthy because an increase in the reported number of meaningful social interactions does not necessarily translate immediately into lower loneliness or higher well-being. Social contact, loneliness, and well-being are related but distinct outcomes, and the short duration and small sample make it difficult to draw conclusions about how they may influence one another.

Cognitive performance

For most of the cognitive measures, changes between the pre-study and post-study assessments did not show an obvious difference in direction or magnitude between the chatbot and control conditions.

Scores on both the Digit Span and Verbal Fluency tests tended to improve from pre- to post-study in both groups. Because the improvements were relatively similar across conditions, one possible explanation is a practice effect: participants may simply have performed better because they had already completed similar tasks during the pre-study assessment.

Digit Symbol Matching

Digit Symbol Matching was the cognitive measure that showed a potentially more noticeable difference in the pattern of pre- to post-study scores between conditions.

However, given the very small number of participants (just one participant for the post-study score), this pattern cannot be interpreted as evidence that chatbot use caused a change in cognitive performance. A larger sample would be needed to determine whether this difference is reproducible or reflects an effect other than that of the intervention.

Key takeaway

Most outcomes did not show clear differences between conditions. The most interesting descriptive finding was an apparent increase in meaningful social interactions in the chatbot group compared with a downward trend in the control group. However, loneliness and well-being remained relatively stable, and most cognitive changes were similar across groups. Because of the very small sample, these findings should be viewed as preliminary patterns that can help guide future research rather than evidence of a reliable chatbot effect.

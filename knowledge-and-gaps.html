What we know—and do not know

Current knowledge in the field & gaps still needing more research

AI chatbots are increasingly capable of natural conversation, but the evidence about long-term social and psychological effects is still developing.

Why AI chatbots are promising

Technology-based interventions may be more scalable, flexible, and cost-effective than some traditional approaches to loneliness (Hansen et al., 2025). Humanlike chatbots such as ChatGPT may also offer accessible, low-pressure conversation.

What recent studies found

Results are mixed. Phang et al. (2025) found that participants reported less loneliness overall after 28 days, but heavier users also tended to socialize less and feel lonelier. Al Mazroui and Alzyoudi (2024) found reduced loneliness in 20 older adults after two weeks of ChatGPT use.

Why we still need caution

Both studies had important limits. Neither included a no-chatbot control group, so we cannot confidently say the chatbot itself caused the changes. Factors such as regression to the mean, participant expectations, or pre-existing loneliness may also have influenced the results.

What broader evidence shows

Gou et al. (2026) reviewed eight randomized controlled trials involving 611 older adults. AI-based agents appeared to improve depressive symptoms, but did not show a significant effect on loneliness.

Can AI support real human connection?

This remains an important research gap. Seaborn et al. (2023) found that voice assistants and humanoid robots may help facilitate conversation among older adults, suggesting AI could potentially support—not replace—real social interaction.

How this connects to our study

These mixed findings motivated our research. We wanted to test whether a voice-based AI chatbot could support loneliness and well-being while also encouraging more meaningful real-life social interaction, using an active audio-diary group for comparison.

✓

Key takeaway

AI chatbots are promising, but the evidence is still mixed. Better controlled studies are needed to understand when they help, who benefits, and whether they can support more real-world human connection.

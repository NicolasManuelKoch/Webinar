Welcome

What was this research about?

Welcome to our webinar about AI chatbots, their potential role as companions for older adults, and the wider influence of artificial intelligence. We created this webinar as a thank-you for taking part in our research, and we hope you enjoy it.

What did we want to find out?

We wanted to examine whether interacting regularly with an AI chatbot could help older adults feel less lonely, improve their well-being, and possibly support aspects of cognitive functioning.

How could a chatbot help?

We expected two possible pathways. First, the conversation itself might provide a positive and supportive experience. Second, the chatbot might act as a social motivator by encouraging participants to speak with other people, go out more often, and seek meaningful social contact.

How was the chatbot guided?

Participants in the AI chatbot group entered a set of instructions that encouraged the chatbot to focus on companionship and social motivation. The instructions were not intended to be read by participants, because knowing the study's exact expectations could have influenced how they later reported their social interactions.

Why were there two groups?

The AI chatbot group was the main experimental group. To determine whether any changes were truly connected to the chatbot—and not to outside events, study participation, or another unrelated factor—we also needed a comparison, or control, group.

What did the control group do?

The control group kept a daily audio diary. This activity was similar to talking with the chatbot because participants still spoke regularly, but it removed the central feature being tested: receiving a response. Participants in this group talked, but were not talked back to.

What did we measure?

We used scientifically studied questionnaires to assess loneliness and well-being. Participants also reported how many social interactions they had experienced during the previous two weeks.

How did we examine cognition?

Participants completed three brief cognitive tests covering working memory, attention, executive functioning, and verbal fluency. These tasks helped us explore whether either daily activity was associated with cognitive changes.

How were the results compared?

We compared each participant's scores before and after the study. We then compared the amount of change in the AI chatbot group with the amount of change in the audio-diary group to determine whether the groups differed in a meaningful way.

✓

Key takeaway

The study tested whether AI chatbots could support older adults both directly, through conversation, and indirectly, by encouraging more human social interaction—while using an audio-diary group as a fair comparison.
